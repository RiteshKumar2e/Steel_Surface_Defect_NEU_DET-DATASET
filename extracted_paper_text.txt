Abstract
Steel surface defect detection is a central task in intelligent manufacturing,
where the objective is to detect and localize defects that may compromise a
component’s structural integrity, quality, and fitness for service. Conventional
automated inspection performs unreliably under adverse factory conditions,
including uneven illumination, irregular surface texture, and cluttered back-
grounds.This manuscript proposes SteelSense-BiLSTM, a lightweight machine-
learning framework that classifies and localizes steel surface defects using a
Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) architecture. The frame-
work integrates OpenCV-based image preprocessing, handcrafted visual feature
extraction, a feature-to-prompt sequence transformation, and a custom tokenizer
that feeds a lightweight recurrent network. The extracted descriptors span sta-
tistical, edge, morphological, frequency-domain, and texture characteristics.For
large-scale deployment, the framework incorporates High-Performance Com-
puting (HPC) techniques: parallelized preprocessing, batch feature extraction,
optimized data pipelines, and accelerated training and inference. These reduce
computational overhead and enable the processing of high-resolution industrial
1

=== PAGE 2 ===
datasets at scale, rendering the method suitable for smart-manufacturing and
industry 4.0 environments.
The framework computes a series of manual visual features for each image of a
steel surface, namely: average intensity, contrast, entropy, density of edges, mag-
nitude of Sobel gradient, variance of Laplacian, statistical measures of contours,
defect area proportion, GLCM features, and local binary pattern (LBP) textures.
These features, expressed in numeric values, are then converted to structured text
prompts and embedded using a custom tokenizer that converts the visual features
into sequences of model inputs. These embeddings are then fed to a light multi-
layer Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) encoder that adopts a
hybrid pooling strategy, comprising attention pooling, masked max pooling, and
masked mean pooling, thereby taking advantage of both fine-grained interactions
of features and context from global scope, and then predicts the classification
head of defects. For scale, feature extraction, processing, and execution of model,
benefit from HPC in order to speed up processing large amounts of industrial
images in parallel, and shorten the training time and inference time, without
reducing classification accuracy.

1 Introduction
Steel is widely being used in all important sectors including automotive, railway,
aerospace and civil and heavy constructions because of its increased strength and
toughness and extensive usage possibilities. In relation to steels surface appearance it
is imperative to mention the most prominent and most important feature of quality,
performance and service life of steel products i.e. Its surface integrity. There exist a
large variety of defects that often arise on surface appearance of steels due to man-
ufacturing and subsequent handling in the context of rolling, cooling, cutting and
post-processing steps in the industrial process that are most commonly related to ther-
mal stresses, oxidizing processes, residues of raw material ingredients and machining
wear[1] . This problem not only leads to loss of the aesthetic quality of the material,
but it negatively affects the overall product performance and consequently creates an
existential operational risk due to economic consequences of reject materials.In the
process of hot rolling and manufacturing, steel surfaces can experience various defects
due to inclusions, mechanical wear, oxidation, and processing anomalies. The defects
impact the surface finish, negatively affect mechanical properties and may even result
in the rejection of manufactured products. Accordingly, proper recognition and clas-
sification of these defects on the steel surface are required for the preservation of the
quality of manufactured products in the industrial setting. Various defects that are
present on the surface of rolled steel products can be grouped into classes including
inclusion, crazing, patch, pitted surface, rolled-in scale, and scratches [2]. Crazing can
manifest as a series of shallow, crack-like imperfections, while inclusions are localized

zones or patches on the surface that are composed of inclusions of impurity mate-
rial [2]. The patches can appear as irregular surface areas that lack a uniform texture
and are locally discolored. Small localized cavities form during steel sheet production
resulting in the occurrence of the pitted surface phenomenon. Rolled-in scale is caused
when oxidised surfaces embed themselves into the material during the rolling process,
and scratches are the result of contact with tooling or rollers during processing [3].
Classical image processing techniques such as thresholding, edge detection, contour
extraction, and morphological operations were previously broadly used to automati-
cally inspect surface defects of steel. Thresholding aims to differentiate defect regions
from the background; edge detection aims to enhance the boundary information of
defect, and contour extraction is used to extract information about the shape and loca-
tion of defects and morphological operations aim to remove noises and make segmented
regions smoother, as shown in Fig. 1.

Conventional CNN-based approaches, on the other hand, often require massive
annotated datasets and intensive training routines. Such reliance makes them imprac-
tical to deploy in environments with limited computing capacity and inefficient once a
manufacturing enterprise expands to any meaningful scale. To this end, we present the
HPC-enabled SteelSense-BiLSTM, a lightweight machine learning paradigm designed
to avoid both issues. To accomplish this task, it combines image preprocessing,

=== PAGE 4 ===
handcrafted feature extraction, feature-to-prompt conversion, makeup tokenization,
attention-based BiLSTM sequence modelling, multi-view pooling, and hybrid defect
localization into an integrated High-Performance Computing (HPC) architecture. The
core HPC idea is to optimize the execution speed without sacrificing classification
precision by means of parallel computing, GPU acceleration, and optimized data
pipelines. The pipeline begins with OpenCV-based preprocessing and handcrafted fea-
ture extraction. For each steel surface image, it computes a set of statistical, edge,
morphological, frequency-domain, and texture descriptors. These operations run under
HPC-based parallel execution, so large industrial image sets can be handled with-
out a bottleneck at the feature stage. The numerical descriptors are then converted
into structured feature prompts and encoded with a custom tokenizer, which repre-
sents each image’s visual characteristics as a sequence of feature tokens. A lightweight
BiLSTM network processes the resulting token embeddings, augmented with three
pooling operations: attention pooling, masked max pooling, and masked mean pooling.
This hybrid pooling captures both the local detail that distinguishes one defect from
another and the broader relationships among features across the sequence, while train-
ing and inference run on GPU-accelerated HPC platforms. The framework is trained
to classify the six defect categories of the NEU-DET & SteelDefectX datasets: crazing,
inclusion, patches, pitted surface, rolled-in scale, and scratches. Localization is per-
formed in two ways. Where XML annotations are available, the framework parses them
to generate accurate bounding boxes. Where they are absent, a contour-based region
detector serves as a fallback, estimating the defect regions so that a visual result is
still produced [4]. High-Performance Computing is what keeps the design from coming
apart at the seams. It maintains usage of limited resources, models runtime linearly
against input sizes, reduces training runtime and accelerates inference time – features
which SteelSense-BiLSTM needs to have to be effective in real time production line
inspection, as well as in the rapidly expanding smart manufacturing market.
The primary contributions of this research include the following:
• In this manuscript, we propose a hybrid model named SteelSense-BiLSTM, a
lightweight and local model without using huge vision-language models for steel
surface defect classification and localization.
• Develop a handcrafted feature extraction framework with various types of
visual descriptors such as statistical, edge, morphological, frequency domain, and
texture ones to describe the steel surface defects in multiple perspectives.
• Introduce a feature-to-prompt transformation approach to encode handcrafted
visual descriptors into a sequence of discrete text tokens for effective sequential
modeling.
• Build a lightweight and customed BiLSTM classifier with an efficient tokenizer
and a multi-view pooling mechanism to precisely classify six different kinds of
steel surface defects in the NEU-DET & SteelDefectX datasets.

2 Literature Review
Steel surface defect detection has been a long standing active research field since that
the manufacturing process is prone to the generation of various defects that can prop-
agate to the final steel product and consequently reduce its mechanical performance,
structural reliability, corrosion resistance, durability and value. Several defects such
as crazing, inclusions, patches, pitted surfaces, rolled-in scales, and scratches on the
steel surface will greatly impair the quality and reliability of steel products, especially
in demanding applications such as automobiles, aerospace, railways, shipbuilding, and
building construction. Accordingly, it is critical to detect the defects on the steel sur-
face effectively and efficiently to ensure product quality, waste reduction, cost saving,
and improved manufacturing performance. Under the trend of intelligence manufac-
turing and Industry 4.0, numerous researches have focused on developing automated,
robust, and efficient inspection systems for the steel surface. The existing defect detec-
tion techniques on the steel surface can be roughly grouped into four major categories:
manual inspection, conventional image processing techniques, Machine Learning (ML)-
based techniques and Deep Learning (DL) based techniques. Each type of detection
technique has played an important role in advancing the field of automated inspec-
tion systems for defect detection with varying capabilities and challenges in detection
accuracy, computation, interpretability and application in practice.
The manual inspection method has been used for industrial manufacturing for
many years due to ease to implement and without the need for high-tech machines
and technician knowledge. It inspects steel surfaces manually by humans and looks
for any surface defects with their knowledge and experience. For small quantities of
products, manual inspection is cheaper, but nowadays, in high-speed manufacturing,
this method is not so effective. The major reason is that it requires a huge number
of person. This method consumes time and money and can be limited due to human
fatigue. Human perception of defects is not consistent and subjective. A defect which
is small and low contrast or very complex in nature might be missed, or the same
pattern might be identified as different defects by two inspectors. In large-volume
manufacturing, manual inspection cannot be scaled up easily, repeated exactly and
make decision immediately, which leads to bad quality, high cost, material wastage
and defective products. For this reason, more and more research has been made for
using computer vision with ML and DL methods to realize automation inspection,
which has high speed, high consistency, high objectivity, and high accuracy to make
intelligent manufacturing possible.
Conventional image processing tackles defect inspection with manually designed
algorithms that read image intensity, texture, edges, and geometry. The approach
rests on handcrafted rules and predefined feature extraction to find defect regions.
Song and Yan [5] proposed a noise-robust detection method built on Completed Local
Binary Patterns (CLBP), which held up well on noisy industrial images. Gonzalez
and Woods developed the basic thresholding, edge detection, contour extraction, and
morphological operators used ever since in industrial inspection. Thresholding distin-
guishes the defect from the background. Edge detection refines boundaries. Contour
extraction approximates the shape and position of a defect. Morphological operators

ML raised steel defect detection by combining hand-made visual features with com-
mon classifiers, while traditional image processing processes the input based solely
on predetermined rules, ML approaches determine the decision boundaries of clas-
sifications using the extracted features. ML classification accuracy has accordingly
improved. Gonget al.[1] used a Multiple Hyperspheres Support Vector Machine
(MHSVM), an extended Support Vector Machine (SVM) that extracts more valuable
discriminative information. SVM, Random Forests, k-Nearest Neighbors, and Decision
Tree algorithms also obtained considerable classification accuracies when applied on
thoughtfully designed statistical features, textures and shape features, as compared
to raw image processing. These techniques are typically more robust and more accu-
rate compared with pure image processing approaches, but rely strongly on features
hand-crafted from the data. Handmade features typically fail to accurately learn sub-
tle defect patterns or adapt to changes in scale, lighting, appearance, and texture. In
recent years, this limits brought the community towards a new deep learning approach
that automatically learns hierarchical features from raw input data directly. The use
of machine learning algorithms for steel defect detection has dominated traditional
methods over the past few years due to its capability to learn effective features directly
from raw input data, without manual engineering. Most progress relies on shared com-
mon learning architectures, and especially residual learning [6] which allows learning
deep networks. In the context of steel surface defect detection, Lvet al.[3] intro-
duced an end-to-end network to learn defect features and localized them on the image
while also providing the classification results; also a famous steel surface defect detec-
tion datasets NEU-DET [2] & SteelDefectX [7] was released, becoming a widely used
benchmark in the field. An alternative direction is to learn joint local and global fea-
tures in the detection process to better conform to practical defect detection tasks.
Further, combining multiple learned deep representations can obtain significant clas-
sification improvement, especially under non-ideal imaging conditions, as reported by
Liet al.[8].
Deep learning object detectors are now extensively deployed for the detection of
steel surface defects, primarily due to their capability to perform localization and clas-
sification simultaneously, with high accuracy. Amongst the most popular deep object
detectors, Ren et al. ’s [9] Faster R-CNN improves localization accuracy with the use
of a Region Proposal Network (RPN) which proposes regions at a lower computational
cost. Redmon et al. ’s [10] YOLO transforms object detection into a single-shot regres-
sion task which provides real-time performance whilst retaining comparable accuracy.
In order to detect objects at different scales and resolutions Lin et al. [11] devised the
Feature Pyramid Network (FPN) by integrating shallow spatial resolution with deep
semantic level information to create multi-scale features which capture small objects
or objects at multiple resolutions efficiently. To reduce computational and memory
usage significantly Sandler et al. [12] created MobileNetV2, an efficient mobile-friendly
CNN with inverted residual blocks and linear bottlenecks which maintains high predic-
tive accuracy. These attributes ensure MobileNetV2’s suitability for computationally

However, more recently work have begun focusing on developing Machine Learn-

deployed to real industrial applications. It was stated by Huanget al.[13] and Wang

and model interpretability, Simultaneously, high-resolution industrial image datasets
continues to increase rapidly and consequently makes inspection system require larger
computational expenses. For the aim to accelerate Machine Learning workflows, HPC
is important that helps efficiently process datasets, paralleling feature extraction and
quicken model training as well as low-latency model inference when large-scale datasets
is involved.Motivated by these objectives, this paper is presents an HPC-enabled
steel defect inspection system, named HPC-enabled SteelSense-BiLSTM which is a
lightweight feature-prompt learning framework that classifies and localize defects on
steel surfaces. Instead of the typical methods for direct image pixel operating in high-
dimension, it firstly process the images by OpenCV based Preprocessing and extract
visual descriptors that represent different features like: Statistical, edge-based, mor-
phology, frequency domain, and textural, that these handcrafted visual descriptors
are then converting into structured feature-prompts and finally, these visual prompts
were encoded by using our customized tokenizer so the information could represent
as tokens sequence for sequence model to process. The resulting token embedding
then fed to attention augmented BiLSTM model with multi-view pooling to effec-
tively capture the Local discriminative pattern and the global context of extracted
features. The developed model further leverages HPC capabilities for parallel Prepro-
cessing, computational-efficient feature extraction and for training as well as inference
via GPU accelerated computation which greatly reduces computational overhead for
scaled inspecting of the massive industrial data and therefore maintains a healthy
trade-off between inspection efficiency and detection performance and which can be
very valuable for steel surface defect inspection in the future.

3.1.1 Datasets Description
The proposed framework was evaluated on two publicly available steel surface defect
datasets, namely NEU-DET [2] that has 1800 images and SteelDefectX [7] that has
1631 images . The NEU-DET dataset consists of six representative steel surface defect
categories: crazing, inclusion, patches, pitted surface, rolled-in scale, and scratches.
Although the SteelDefectX dataset contains a broader collection of 24 defect cate-
gories with diverse industrial surface conditions, only the six defect categories common
to the NEU-DET dataset (crazing, inclusion, patches, pitted surface, rolled-in scale,
and scratches) were selected for this study to ensure a fair and consistent comparative
evaluation across both datasets. These defect categories represent some of the most fre-
quently encountered surface imperfections in steel manufacturing and pose significant
challenges for automated industrial inspection systems [8]. Prior to feature extraction
and model evaluation, all images were resized to a resolution of 200×200 pixels to
maintain a uniform input representation and reduce computational complexity.
3.1.2 Surface Defect Categories
The NEU-DET & SteelDefectX datasets contains six different classes of commonly
occurring defects on steel surfaces. Each defect represents a different process defect
that commonly occur in an industrial setting.
• Crazing (Cr):Crazing is a random pattern of fine-cracked lines found across
the surface of steel that is produced as a result of thermal stress, extreme cooling
and fatigue on the material during the rolling process and causes damage to the
overall properties of the steel.
• Inclusion(In):Inclusion defects are derived from foreign non-metallic matter,
such as oxides, sulfides, slag which has been entrained into the steel while being
made. This type of defect seems like irregularly distributed white and black region
and might weaken the physical strength.
• Patches (Pa):Patch defects are regions where local variations in texture or gray
level appear due to some local disturbance on the surface, such as oxidation, the
accumulation of a foreign deposit or a coating defect. They are normally irregular
in shape and therefore difficult to distinguish from the local surface context.
• Pitted Surface (PS):Pitted surfaces are small holes, pits or pits created by
such factors as chemical corrosion, oxidation or the damages induced during fab-
rication and processing. These pits appear randomly over the surfaces in various
size ranges.
• Rolled-in Scale (RS):Rolled-in scale is defined as defects created by inclusion
of the scale which is formed during the high-temperature production process into
the surface of the steel sheet during hot rolling. The shape of these defects is
elongated, irregular, and with a rough surface.
• Scratches (Sc):Scratches are defined as long and narrow channels which can be
introduced by the friction that exists between tools, rollers or parts of any machin-
ery involved in the movement and processing of steel product. Such scratches are
usually of long aspect ratio and have strong directionality.

3.2 Proposed Method
This manuscript proposes HPC-enabled SteelSense-BiLSTM, a lightweight feature-
prompt learning framework for steel surface defect classification and localization.
Unlike conventional Convolutional Neural Network (CNN)-based approaches that
directly learn feature representations from raw images, the proposed framework first
extracts handcrafted visual descriptors and transforms them into structured feature
prompts for sequential representation learning. These prompts are tokenized by a cus-
tom tokenizer, and then fed into an attention-equipped Bidirectional Long Short-Term
Memory (BiLSTM) network for defect classification. Afterwards, a hybrid localization
method including XML annotations and contour-based region detection is applied to
detect defect regions.
The HPC techniques are utilized throughout the computational pipeline for effi-
cient handling of large-scale industrial inspection. Parallel image preprocessing and
handcrafted feature extraction speed up the processing of high-resolution steel surface
images. Meanwhile, GPU-based model training and inference further decrease com-
putational time to an acceptable level for industrial application. By this means, the
presented HPC-aware design guarantees efficient usage of computation resources and
scalability and offers good prospects for real-time intelligent manufacturing.
The overall methodology consists of the following five sequential stages:
A. Image Preprocessing
B. Handcrafted feature extraction
C. Feature-to-prompt transformation
D. Custom tokenization and HPC-accelerated BiLSTM classification
E. Hybrid defect localization

3.2.1 Image Preprocessing
Preprocessing of the images in parallel is the initial part of the proposed framework.
Since the images of the steel surface is usually taken from the industrial production
lines, various lightnings conditions, camera setting, sensor parameters can generate
image quality and image characteristic disparity that cause differences between the
extracted features and deteriorate the performance of the latter defect detection stages.
In order to handle such issue, we have established an image preprocessing pipeline
that aims to normalize the images and reduce image acquired noise before any feature
extraction. As the processing of individual images is completely independent from the
other, the whole preprocessing pipeline is a data-level parallel pipeline. Thus, we set
this pipeline on the HPC environment to process multiple images in parallel, so that
the preprocessing step won’t bottleneck when working with large volume of images.
The processing pipeline comprises four subsequent steps which are presented in
the next paragraphs: image loading, resizing, gray-level scaling and Gaussian filtering.
Initially, the loaded images are re-sampled to a standardized dimension of 200×200
pixels through the bilinear interpolation method. Through this step the dimensions of
the samples, irrespective to their source, will conform a same format. Afterwards, the
scaled images of type RGB will be translated into grayscale. This conversion will omit
the information about color which for this application is irrelevant, and it helps the
detection process to minimize redundant visual information for the detection process.
A Gaussian 5×5 filter with a standard deviation ofσ= 1.0 is finally applied for
the suppression of high frequency sensor noise and minor imaging artifacts without
considerably affecting the defect edges. All the previously applied preprocessing stages
like reading, resizing, conversion to grayscale, applying Gaussian filter are run in the
parallel HPC-pipeline. Since each image is processed separately, an increase in pro-
cessing cores implies an increase in processing throughput which is of great interest
for processing industrial datasets.

3.2.2 Handcrafted Feature Extraction
After preprocessing, features are extracted by hand rather than learned from raw
pixels. A conventional end-to-end CNN would infer its own representations during
training; here the descriptors are defined in advance, each one capturing a specific
statistical, edge, texture, or geometric property of a defect region. Two things follow
from this choice. The features stay interpretable, since every value corresponds to a
named measurement, and the input to the later sequence model is far smaller than a
raw image, which lowers the cost of that stage.
Feature extraction on one image does not depend on any other, so the computation
is distributed across multiple cores on a High-Performance Computing (HPC) setup.
Running the images in parallel raises throughput enough to handle large industrial
datasets without changing what is computed per image.

(c) Texture Features.
Texture is often what distinguishes two defect classes that are otherwise similar in
appearance. To capture both global and local texture, descriptors are drawn from the
Gray Level Co-occurrence Matrix (GLCM) and from Local Binary Patterns (LBP).
The GLCM features — contrast, homogeneity, energy, and correlation — describe how
neighboring pixel intensities relate across the image [18]. LBP captures fine local pat-
terns and remains robust under varying illumination, which is relevant on an inspection
line where lighting is not fixed [19].
(d) Geometric Features.
Contour analysis provides shape information. For each detected contour, the frame-
work computes area, perimeter, aspect ratio, and circularity [17]. These descriptors
help distinguish elongated defects such as scratches from irregular ones such as patches
and crazing. Together, the statistical, edge-based, texture, and geometric descriptors
form an interpretable representation of steel surface defects. The handcrafted features

3.2.3 Feature-to-Prompt Transformation
The handcrafted feature extraction stage yields a compact numerical representation of
the statistical, textural, edge, and geometric characteristics of each steel surface image.
While these descriptors adequately characterize the visual properties of surface defects,
they are not inherently amenable to sequence-based learning. The framework therefore
employs a feature-to-prompt transformation, which maps the numerical feature vector
onto a structured textual representation while preserving its semantic content.
Rather than operating on high-dimensional pixel data, the method encodes each
defect as a sequence of descriptive feature tokens. Continuous-valued descriptors,
namely entropy, edge density, Sobel gradient magnitude, GLCM texture measures,
LBP descriptors, and contour-based geometric properties, are discretized into pre-
defined semantic categories in accordance with their respective threshold ranges.
Each such descriptor is assigned an interpretable token, such as Low, Medium or
High, whereas categorical texture and geometric descriptors are mapped directly to
predefined semantic labels. This procedure transforms heterogeneous numerical mea-
surements into a unified sequential representation amenable to processing by the
subsequent BiLSTM model.
As each feature vector is transformed independently, the conversion stage admits
parallel execution across images, the HPC pipeline prompt generation is parallelized
over the datasets, thereby improving throughput at negligible computational cost.
The representation consequently remains computationally efficient even for large-scale
industrial inspection tasks.

the proposed approach provides a scalable and computationally efficient representation
for large-scale industrial steel surface inspection.

3.2.4 Custom tokenization and HPC-accelerated BiLSTM
classification
Structured textual prompts created by feature-to-prompt transformation stage are
converted by our designed tokenization approach so as to enable sequence based
learning. Because the feature prompts generated are textual feature description of sta-
tistical, texture, edge, geometric features they need to be represented by numerical
form for the purpose of being processed by the neural networks. Our designed tok-
enization represents a prompt into a sequence of tokens such that semantic connections
of the feature descriptors within a prompt is maintained.
A regular-expression-based tokenizer is utilized to recognize single tokens in each
feature prompt and map them to a target vocabulary. The tokens are represented
by integers to create sequences of indices which maintain their order of occurrence.
Because prompts usually consist of different number of tokens, the sequences are either
padded or truncated to a maximum fixed length to maintain uniformity across the
samples which is a requirement of the batch-wise data input. In the High-Performance
Computing (HPC) architecture, tokenization and the creation of sequences is per-
formed in parallel on multiple samples to accelerate the preprocessing time required
for data preparing.

3.2.5 Hybrid Defect Localization
Defect localization is the fundamental component in the automated steel surface
inspection system because it not only gives defect type, but also detects the spatial
coordinate of defects in the images. The good localization allows human eye observa-
tion, defect size measurement, and subsequent production quality control. Therefore,
this work proposes a hybrid localization technique for realizing robust localization
under varying annotation condition: uses XML labels to localize defect when they
exist, and switch to contour based regional detect method.
For images containing XML annotation files, the proposed framework extracts
the ground-truth bounding-box coordinates directly from the annotations provided
in the NEU-DET [2] & SteelDefectX[7] datasets . The extracted coordinates are
rescaled according to the resized image dimensions to preserve the spatial corre-
spondence between the annotation and the processed image. This approach provides
accurate localization for fully annotated samples while maintaining consistency with
the preprocessing stage.
In absence of annotation files, the framework provides a method for automatically
localizing potential regions by estimating candidates based on the contour of the object

edge detection is then thresholded, combined using morphology operations in order to

region of candidate defects and its bounding boxes are computed based on the defined
geometrical restrictions.
Since localization for each image is independent, the proposed High-Performance
Computing (HPC) pipeline processes multiple images concurrently. This parallel exe-
cution significantly reduces localization time for large-scale industrial datasets while
maintaining the accuracy of the detected defect regions.

4.1 Experimental Setup
This subsection describes the technical details of the experiments carried out to
evaluate the proposed SteelSense-BiLSTM framework. It includes the implementa-
tion environment, feature processing pipeline, datasets partitioning strategy, and
evaluation protocol adopted for steel surface defect classification and localization.
4.1.1 Hardware and Software Configuration
All experiments are performed on a workstation with an Intel Core i7 processor and
16GB of RAM with GPU that includes 8GB of memory by Python 3.11 language.
Image preprocessing steps were achieved by the OpenCV library; handcraft feature
was extracted using NumPy and Scikit-image library, and the proposed SteelSense-
BiLSTM was developed and trained by PyTorch library. All experiments were run in
an operating system like Windows 11. We also enable the GPU acceleration through
the CUDA framework to make the model training and inference faster.
Following handcrafted feature extraction, the numerical descriptors were trans-
formed into structured feature prompts using the proposed feature-to-prompt transfor-
mation strategy. The generated prompts were subsequently processed by the custom
tokenizer, which converted them into fixed-length integer sequences through padding
and truncation before they were provided as input to the proposed SteelSense-BiLSTM
classifier.
To improve computational efficiency, the preprocessing, handcrafted feature
extraction, feature-to-prompt transformation, and tokenization stages ran on an
HPC-enabled pipeline. Each image passes through these stages independently, so pro-
cessing them in parallel cut preprocessing overhead and raised throughput on large
steel-surface datasets.

4.6 Discussion
The results show that the proposed SteelSense-BiLSTM framework delivers good
robustness and stability in automating the classification of steel surface defects using
the NEU-DET & SteelDefectX datasets. Leveraging handcrafted feature selection and
sequence learning allows the network to clearly differentiate the styles of the defects
with improved detection accuracy at low system load, which meets demands in the
industrial sector. Over the entire training procedure, the curves for training and val-
idation are shown to be stable convergence. They show that no over-fitting can be
obviously observed during training or after testing.
A significant observation from the experimental validation is that the feature
prompt based representation by formulating handcrafted descriptors as prompts
(instead of being learned from image pixels directly) is a good idea that provides
the BiLSTM network with the ability to correlate the heterogeneous descriptors such
as statistical, texture, edge, geometric features. Therefore, the framework learned
discriminative feature representations with substantially less trainable parameters
compared to most existing methods that adopt deep convolution or attention.
This proposed strategy of multi-view pooling boosts high quality features still
higher. Our attention pooling strategy learns to extract features by assigning attention
value to relevant feature tokens, and maximum pooling extract local feature of strong
response and average pooling maintain the rich context information among features,
so to obtain richer fused features, which leads to more robust and stable performance
on classification of different types of defects.
The experimental results demonstrated that the proposed framework showed a
steady performance for all the six surface defect classes in the NEU-DET & SteelDe-
fectX datasets. The distinctive feature defects with different structural traits can still
be recognized confidently, and similar defects can also get compatible detection ability.
Besides, the balanced value of precision, recall and F1-score on each class illustrated
that the presented model does not have too obvious advantages and disadvantages on
any particular type of defects.
From a computational standpoint, the proposed framework provides an appealing
compromise between classifier performance and computational demands. Requiring
only 1.75 Million training parameters, and an overall model size close to 7.89MB,
the framework is far less complex in terms of the sheer number of parameters when
compared to many of the recent deep learning architectures, rendering SteelSense-
BiLSTM efficient for real-time, resource-constrained industrial applications such as
on-site inspection.
One of the key strengths of the proposed SteelSense-BiLSTM framework is its effi-
cient balance between predictive performance and computational complexity. Despite
its lightweight architecture, the framework achieves reliable classification performance
25

=== PAGE 26 ===
while maintaining low memory requirements and fast inference, making it well suited
for resource-constrained industrial inspection systems.
However, there are a number of limitations in the research although there were
satisfactory experimental results. The proposed methodology was tested on a single
benchmark datasets: NEU-DET & SteelDefectX and feature engineering by selecting
manually the features is a pre-defined procedure. Such handmade features demonstrate
good discriminative ability but they possibly may perform differently in varied lighting
situations or production environments. Therefore more results would be desired if
tested on large scaled industrial image database concerning various light intensities,
contaminations and manufacturing factors.
The proposed framework was evaluated on the publicly available NEU-DET [2]
& SteelDefectX [7] datasets, which consists of six representative categories of steel
surface defects. Experimental results demonstrate that the feature-prompt represen-
tation effectively preserves the discriminative characteristics of different defect types,
resulting in high classification and localization performance. To improve the general
applicability of the proposed framework, a hybrid localization strategy was employed.
XML annotations were directly utilized for defect localization whenever bounding-
box labels were available, whereas contour-based localization was adopted for images
without bounding-box annotations. Consequently, the proposed framework can be
effectively applied to both fully annotated and partially annotated datasets [3, 4].
To improve computational efficiency, image preprocessing, handcrafted feature
extraction, feature-prompt generation, and sequence preparation were implemented
using a High-Performance Computing (HPC) pipeline. The stages in this workflow
are designed to run in parallel since each image can be processed separately from the
rest of them, thus allowing high through-put when working with industrial datasets
of millions of images. We are able to process data in parallel with a small overhead,
not affecting the complexity of our model and making it suitable for practical appli-
cations in the industrial environment. Our approach leverages the power of sequence
learning in a light manner, combined with data parallel processing, offering an efficient
and scalable model to the industrial steel surface inspection problem under restricted
computation power and real-time processing constraint [12].
5 Conclusion
This manuscript presented SteelSense-BiLSTM, a lightweight feature-prompt learning
framework for steel surface defect classification and localization. Instead of learning
directly from raw images, the proposed framework extracts handcrafted statistical,
texture, edge, and geometric features [18, 19], transforms them into structured feature
prompts, and models their relationships using an attention-enhanced Bidirectional
Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network [21]. This structure has retained the
interpretability of handmade features and has been supported by learning sequences
for defective classification. The light architecture is designed to have few parame-
ters comparing with other standard deep convolutional neural networks [6, 12], and
therefore, can be deployed in industrial contexts with low computing capabilities.

Our future work involves extending the developed framework to distributed and
multi-GPU environments to enable large-scale training and parallelizing inference
algorithms to optimize them for real-time industrial deployment. Integrating feature
prompt learning with Graph Neural Networks (GNNs) [22, 23], Knowledge Graphs
[24], and Vision-Language Models (VLMs) [25, 26] is also another interesting research
direction that will significantly boost defect representations, contextual reasoning
and semantic understanding, laying a solid foundation for next-generation smart
manufacturing systems to make smarter and more reliable decisions.
